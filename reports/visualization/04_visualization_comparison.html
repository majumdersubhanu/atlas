ATLAS · From contagion engine to visual analytics¶

Mid-term demonstration · 2 November 2026¶

Question: Does an interactive graph help users trace financial-loss propagation more accurately and efficiently than an exposure-matrix heatmap of the same network?

This notebook verifies the experimental foundation. It does not contain user-study results. The proposed participant pool is open recruitment, screened for basic task understanding, with domain experience recorded and financial analysts particularly relevant.

Two comparisons: graph vs matrix changes the visual encoding; direct-loss vs full cascade changes the financial propagation model. A model difference is not a visualization improvement.

Data: fictional banks; amounts in synthetic teaching units

1 · A controlled example with an answer we can check¶

A lends 100 to B. C lends 80 to A. Starting equity is A=50, B=50, C=40. B is forced to fail; recovery is 40%; the external haircut is zero.

  • A loses 100 × 60% = 60, leaving equity −10.
  • C then loses 80 × 60% = 48, leaving equity −8.
  • The full cascade posts 108; the direct-loss control posts 60.

Loan arrows point lender → borrower; losses travel borrower → lender. A forced default can retain positive book equity. All figures are synthetic teaching units.

2 · Same loans, two encodings¶

These displays are built from the same payload. Neither receives extra hidden exposures. The graph below is at round zero: B is the forced initial failure. The matrix contains original loan amounts; it is not a price-correlation matrix. Both are valid views of the same data.

3 · Trace the loss, round by round¶

Positions stay fixed. Each newly failed borrower transmits once, to creditors alive at the start of that round. The ledger distinguishes the initial failure from secondary failures. Rounds are computational steps, not days.

4 · Change one assumption¶

At 50% recovery, A and then C reach exactly zero equity and fail under our threshold. At 51%, A remains solvent and does not propagate. At 100%, there are no interbank losses, but forced B remains an initial failure. These are conditional arithmetic results.
